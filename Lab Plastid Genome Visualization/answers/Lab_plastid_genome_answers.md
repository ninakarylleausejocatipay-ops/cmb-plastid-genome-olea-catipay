# Lab Plastid Genome Visualization — Answers

### 1. Scientific name and plastid genome accession number
The scientific name of my chosen plant is *Olea europaea*, and its plastid genome accession number is NC_013707.2.

### 2. Total length of the plastid genome
The total length of the plastid genome is 155,888 bp.

### 3. LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions
Yes. The LSC region is located at the upper portion of the map (~85,528 bp), while the SSC region is located at the lower portion (~17,856 bp). IRa is located on the right side (~26,252 bp), and IRb is located on the left side (~26,252 bp). The two IR regions separate the LSC and SSC regions.

### 4. Three genes located in the LSC region
Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are *rbcL*, *psbA*, and *atpA*.

### 5. Gene located in the SSC region
One example of a gene located in the SSC region is *ndhF*.

### 6. Gene located within an inverted repeat region
One example is *rrn16* (or *rrn23*). Yes, it occurs more than once because the two inverted repeat regions contain duplicated copies of genes.

### 7. Photosynthesis-related gene
The *rbcL* gene is a photosynthesis-related gene that encodes the large subunit of Rubisco. Rubisco is involved in carbon fixation during photosynthesis.

### 8. Direction of gene arrows and transcription
The direction of the arrows indicates the direction in which the genes are transcribed along the $5' \rightarrow 3'$ strand. Genes on the outer ring are transcribed counter-clockwise, while genes on the inner ring are transcribed clockwise.

### 9. GC content
The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the plastid genome. The graph shows clear fluctuations, with noticeably higher GC content peaks concentrated in the inverted repeat (IR) regions due to the presence of GC-rich ribosomal RNA genes.

### 10. Usefulness of a graphical plastid genome map
A graphical plastid genome map makes it easier to visualize the genome organization, locations of genes, LSC, SSC, and IR regions, gene orientation, and GC content. It allows structural features and gene distribution to be understood more quickly than from a raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations.
